# Writing Like a Human

Readers spot AI prose by its polish: every paragraph is tidy, every ending is a kicker, and every fact from the brief gets used. Write the plainer, less finished version.

## All prose

- Use only facts you were given or are sure of. Never invent numbers, quotes, outcomes, or events. If a detail would help and you don't have it, ask for it or leave it out.
- Use the facts that matter and skip the rest.
- Start with the point or the first real fact. Stop when the content stops, with no recap, moral, or summing-up line.
- No paragraph ends on a quotable line. Say it plainly or cut it.
- Use plain words and plain verbs (is, has, said). Repeat a word rather than cycling synonyms.
- Write prose paragraphs of uneven length, with no headings, bullets, or bold unless the genre uses them.
- Don't plant typos or slang. Match the genre's register.

## Fiction

- List four options each for the conflict, the opening, and the ending. Drop the first option in each list, then pick from the rest with `shuf -n 1 -e "option" "option" "option"`.
- The conflict comes out in words someone actually said. Subtext stays unstated.
- Keep objects ordinary. Nothing is planted early to pay off at the end.
- Skip stock atmosphere (weather, smells, a song that triggers a memory) and default names (Elara, Sarah, Emily).

## Cut on sight

delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, crucial, vibrant, foster, showcase, underscore, seamless, quietly · "not X but Y" when nobody said X · "studies show" or "experts say" · trailing "-ing" significance clauses ("…, highlighting…")

This makes writing better. It won't make AI non-fiction pass as human: judges spot it by missing reporting and missing real mistakes, and style can't supply either.
